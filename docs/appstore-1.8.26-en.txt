Four fixes found by an independent review of the code. None of them ever produced an error: they were things that happened silently.

ALL OR NOTHING

Every dive that enters the logbook is three writes: the summary, the profile and the optional second profile. They were meant to be a single unit, and they were not: the database engine does not hold a single connection, and the command opening the transaction ended up on a different connection from the one doing the writing.

What changes: if the app is killed by the system while saving — on a phone that happens when memory runs short — the dive is now either in the logbook whole, with its profile, or not there at all. Before, you could be left with a dive without its profile, and on a device with no account that profile never came back. The same applies to deleting a dive and to clearing the logbook.

SYNC NO LONGER SHORTENS A SAVED PROFILE

When the same dive has been downloaded from two computers, the app keeps two profiles. The two travelled together: if the main one had to go up, the second went with it, over a richer second profile already on the other device. Measured: an alternate profile of 100 samples cut down to 5, with no warning.

Now each is decided on its own, and they can go in opposite directions. And the second profile is also downloaded when it is the only one there: before, on a dive with no main profile, it never arrived.

NITROGEN LOADING RIGHT AFTER AN IMPORT

After importing, the saturation card said "a sampled profile is needed" next to a profile that was right there; closing and reopening the app made the number appear. Residual loading, GF99 and the surface interval are not computed from a single dive: they come from the chain between one dive and the next, and that step was missing in exactly the two import paths.

Every fix has a test, and every test was put to the test by putting the defect back: 2,829 automated checks, plus 140 on the native engine.
